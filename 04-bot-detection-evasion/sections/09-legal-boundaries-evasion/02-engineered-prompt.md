# Step 2 — Engineered Prompt (Prompt Engineering.md workflow)
## Course 04 Bot Detection Evasion → Section: Legal Boundaries Around Evasion

The RESULT below is the engineered prompt. In Step 4 this RESULT becomes the task for building the SKILL.md.

RESULT:

Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Legal Boundaries Around Evasion”** as part of an advanced course in bot management literacy, detection signals, and legitimate automation practice. How to think about CFAA-style access law, terms enforcement, trespass, and copyright limits on scraping, when to escalate to counsel, and how to keep decision logs that show restraint (informational, not legal advice).

The material should teach how to recognize legal-risk patterns in collection plans and escalate properly. Go beyond surface-level tips and examine detection-aware legitimate automation as a disciplined practice in which every choice is observable, repeatable, and justified against evidence.

Explain how strong detection outcomes balances **access success, politeness, transparency, permission, and restraint**. Show how these principles apply differently to single-target research, fleet politeness, and access program oversight. Discuss how the techniques in this module compose into one coherent workflow rather than disconnected tricks.

Teach how to spot access-law risk patterns such as bypassing technical barriers and exceeding granted scope

Teach how to read terms for automation, aggregation, and account-action clauses

Teach how to separate plainly public data from gated, paywalled, or account-bound data

Teach how to escalate to counsel with tight briefs and stop-work triggers

Teach how to keep decision logs that demonstrate restraint and review

Include practical methods for real projects. Cover:

* Risk-pattern spotting
* Terms-clause mapping
* Public-versus-gated tests
* Counsel-brief drafting
* Stop-work triggers
* Decision logging
* Jurisdiction noting
* Review cadences

Explain how practitioners can avoid terms ambiguous (vague automation language).
Explain how practitioners can avoid public but rate-walled (public data with technical friction).
Explain how practitioners can avoid jurisdiction unclear (global targets, local team).

Use numerous realistic examples throughout the module. For each important principle, show weak, improved, and professional-level examples where useful. Examples should cover p, r, o, t, e, c, t, e, d,  , s, t, o, r, e, f, r, o, n, t, s, ,,  , r, a, t, e, -, l, i, m, i, t, e, d,  , A, P, I, s, ,,  , l, o, g, i, n, -, g, a, t, e, d,  , p, o, r, t, a, l, s, ,,  , m, e, d, i, a,  , s, i, t, e, s, ,,  , a, n, d,  , p, u, b, l, i, c,  , d, a, t, a,  , p, o, r, t, a, l, s. Keep examples focused on detection outcomes rather than generic advice.

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:

1. Spot risks in three scraping plans.
2. Map terms clauses for two targets.
3. Classify ten datasets public vs gated.
4. Draft one counsel brief.
5. Define stop-work triggers.
6. Keep one decision log for a month.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with fundamentals and moving toward advanced professional practice. Establish clear conceptual distinctions before showing how concepts interact.

Use professional terminology from bot management, detection signals, challenge handling, crawl politeness, and access negotiation where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize bot-management vendor docs, IETF robots and rate-limit standards, measurement research, and platform access guidance. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, content farms, or unsupported “best practice” claims.

Create a professional framework that a practitioner could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, practical reference, and working methodology.

Where a recommendation depends on context, explicitly explain the trade-off rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with a **risk-spot checklist**, a **terms-map checklist**, a **public-test checklist**, a **brief-ready checklist**, a **log-kept checklist** that can be used by a professional team before work is approved for production.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in practical work.
