# Step 2 — Engineered Prompt (Prompt Engineering.md workflow)
## Course 15 Ethical Scraping → Section: Incident Ethics: Stop, Disclose, Remediate

The RESULT below is the engineered prompt. In Step 4 this RESULT becomes the task for building the SKILL.md.

---

RESULT:

Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Incident Ethics: Stop, Disclose, Remediate”** as part of an advanced course in ethical scraping, social license, and responsible data operations. How stop criteria, harm assessment, disclosure duties, and remediation with amends handle scraping incidents ethically — from over-collection to exposure.

The material should teach how to handle scraping incidents ethically. Go beyond surface-level tips and examine responsible operations as a disciplined practice in which every choice is observable, repeatable, and justified against evidence.

Explain how strong ethical posture balances **value, harm, consent, fairness, and trust**. Show how these principles apply differently to single scripts, team practices, and organizational culture. Discuss how the techniques in this module compose into one coherent workflow rather than disconnected tricks.

Teach how to set stop criteria (triggers, authority, drills) before incidents

Teach how to stop collection instantly with kill-switches and scope freezes

Teach how to assess harm (who affected, how badly, spreading?) quickly

Teach how to disclose to affected parties and public appropriately

Teach how to remediate beyond minimum with amends and prevention

Include practical methods for real projects. Cover:

* Stop criteria
* Kill switches
* Harm triage
* Disclosure tiers
* Amends design
* Prevention tasks
* Blameless review
* Trust tracking

Explain how practitioners can avoid slow stop (no kill-switch).
Explain how practitioners can avoid disclosure dread (reputation fear).
Explain how practitioners can avoid repeat incidents (review rot).

Use numerous realistic examples throughout the module. For each important principle, show weak, improved, and professional-level examples where useful. Examples should cover p, u, b, l, i, c,  , w, e, b, ,,  , m, a, r, k, e, t, p, l, a, c, e, s, ,,  , s, o, c, i, a, l,  , p, l, a, t, f, o, r, m, s, ,,  , r, e, s, e, a, r, c, h,  , c, o, r, p, o, r, a, ,,  , a, n, d,  , A, I,  , d, a, t, a, s, e, t, s. Keep examples focused on ethical posture rather than generic advice.

Include practical exercises that require the learner to:

1. Set criteria once.
2. Drill one stop.
3. Triage one sim.
4. Draft one disclosure.
5. Design one amends.
6. Review blamelessly.

For each exercise, provide the scenario, objective, constraints, expected deliverable, evaluation criteria, and an expert-quality example solution where appropriate.

Structure the material progressively, beginning with fundamentals and moving toward advanced professional practice. Establish clear conceptual distinctions before showing how concepts interact.

Use professional terminology from stakeholders, harms, consent, fairness, and accountability where relevant, but explain specialized terminology in clear language. Do not make the material sound like generic AI-generated advice.

Research the subject using high-quality professional and academic sources where external research materially strengthens the material. Prioritize research ethics (Belmont/Menlo), ACM/IEEE codes, robots conventions, and AI data ethics references. Avoid relying primarily on low-quality SEO articles, content farms, or unsupported “best practice” claims.

Create a professional framework that a practitioner could actually use in a real project. The final material should therefore function simultaneously as a learning resource, practical reference, and working methodology.

Where a recommendation depends on context, explicitly explain the trade-off rather than presenting it as an absolute rule.

End the module with a **criteria-1 checklist**, a **drilled-1 checklist**, a **triaged-1 checklist**, a **drafted-1 checklist**, a **reviewed-1 checklist** that can be used by a professional team before work is approved for production.

The final result should be comprehensive enough to serve as an advanced professional training module, but organized clearly enough that a learner can study it progressively and apply each concept in practical work.
