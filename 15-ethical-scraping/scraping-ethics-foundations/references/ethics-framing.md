# Ethics Framing

Stakeholders: data subjects (powerless-first analysis), site operators (costs, ToS, relationships), downstream users (decisions your data feeds), public (norms, trust), your team (moral load). Map per project with vulnerability noted; powerless stakeholders get extra weight.

Harms: privacy (exposure, aggregation, re-identification), economic (bandwidth, competition, devaluation), autonomy (manipulation, chilling), dignitary (misrepresentation, context violation), ecosystem (arms races, commons depletion). Score likelihood x severity; write worst cases plainly; uncertainty widens precautions.

Benefits must be real and evidenced, not speculative; least-harm alternatives exhausted first (APIs, samples, aggregates, licensed data). Consent ladder: direct > opt-out > proxy/community > transparency-only — climb as high as feasible; document where you land and why.

Decisions: written memos (facts, stakes, alts, consent, weighing, dissent, review date); dissent protected and recorded; review on change; publish methods and limits aptly. Legality floors, ethics ceilings. Belmont/Menlo principles (respect, beneficence, justice) as compass.
